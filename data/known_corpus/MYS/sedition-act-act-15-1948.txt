Table of contents 3. 5 A "seditious tendency" is defined in a broad and vague manner, and includes wordings like “bring into hatred or contempt or to excite disaffection against any Ruler or against any Government”, “to raise discontent or disaffection” amongst the population, "to promote feelings of ill will and hostility between different races or classes of the population", and "to question any matter, right, status, position, privilege, sovereignty or prerogative” related to the Malay language as the national language, the special privileges accorded to ethnic Malays and Natives of Sabah and Sarawak (otherwise known as Bumiputera), and the sovereignty of the Rulers. However, speech or acts that merely intend to point out mistakes, errors of defects in any of the measures or decisions of the Ruler or the Government are not considered to be seditious by this reason only. 5 4.(1) 7 This section makes it an offence to perform, prepare or conspire to do an act which has a seditious tendency; to utter any seditious words,and to print, sell, publish, distribute, reproduce or import any seditious publication. The punishment is prescribed is a fine and/or imprisonment up to three years for the first offence. 7 (2) 7 This section criminalizes those who have any seditious publication in their possession without lawful excuse and prescribes a punishment of fine and/or imprisonment of up to eighteen months for the first offence. 7 Sedition Act 1948 [Act 15] Law Status of Law Final/Active/Current Country Malaysia Country Location Latitude : 4.2105 Longitude : 101.9758 Country Location ( linked Law ) Malaysia Keywords Freedom of Expression Sedition Adoption Date Jul 19, 1948 Type of Law Primary (this category will be deleted) Research Category II Fundamental Rights and Freedoms Is this the Official Copy?
