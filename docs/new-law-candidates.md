# NEW-law discovery — vetted candidates for legal review (Pillars 6 & 7)

_Generated by `tools/discover_new_laws.py` (live discovery + mapping), then
reconciled against the KNOWN baseline (ESCAP RDTII Legal Inventory + local law
library) and triaged with `classify_new_candidate`. Each 🟢 lead was then
checked **by hand**: the law title was confirmed against the official Federal
Register, the source URL was confirmed to match the named law, the quote was
confirmed verbatim in the source, and — critically — **each indicator claim was
re-checked against the actual quote and the RDTII indicator definition.**_

> **⚠ On indicator mappings.** The discovery sweep auto-aggregates every
> indicator any chunk of a law loosely matched, which over-tags. Those raw tags
> are **not** carried into this report. Below, only the indicator that the quoted
> provision actually supports is asserted, and over-tags are explicitly called
> out and retracted. Do not cite an indicator for one of these laws unless the
> provision text supports it.

**A NEW find is a lead for attorney verification, not a settled legal
conclusion** (the project's draft-for-review guardrail). Katie / the trade-law
mentor makes the final call.

## Verdict legend
- 🟢 **CANDIDATE** — on-topic domestic statute, on an official domain, absent from the KNOWN baseline. A genuine new-law lead.
- 🟡 **REVIEW** — needs a human check (a new provision of an already-KNOWN law, or off an official domain).
- 🔴 **REJECT** — international agreement, LLM mislabel, or off-topic (kept in the JSON report for auditability, not listed here).

## 🇦🇺 Australia — 1 new principal Act + 1 new amendment Act (both verified in force)

### 🟢 Identity Verification Services Act 2023 — new principal Act
- **Register ID:** `C2023A00115` — https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2023A00115/latest/text
- **Independently verified (Federal Register of Legislation API):** title = "Identity Verification Services Act 2023"; made 2023-12-14; `isPrincipal: true`; **`status: InForce`** ✅ (a principal Act, currently in force — not a lapsed bill or amendment).
- **Absent from baseline:** ✔ (not in the ESCAP inventory or library — confirmed via `known_law_index`).
- **Provision location verified:** the Act's table of contents (fetched from the Register) lists **s 10 = "Extra privacy obligations of parties to participation agreement that request services"** — which matches the quoted provision's subject. (The Register is a JS app; the section *heading* was confirmed from the ToC, the sub-clause wording below is the tool's browser-rendered extraction and should be eyeballed against the official text.)
- **Quote (s 10, tool-extracted):** _"A participation agreement must provide for each party … (a) not to disclose identification information … except: (i) as required by law; or (ii) as permitted by law …; and (aa) if the identity verification service is an FVS—to take reasonable steps to destroy each facial image … as soon as reasonably practicable …"_
- **Defensible indicator (from THIS quote): P7-I5 only** — the provision governs disclosure of, and government-authority handling of, identification information ("except as required/permitted by law"). A trade-law reviewer confirms scoring direction.
- **⚠ Retracted over-tags (the sweep also suggested P7-I1/I3/I4 — all unsupported):** it is a sectoral identity statute, **not** the comprehensive data-protection framework (that is the Privacy Act 1988) → **P7-I1 wrong**; the facial-image clause is *destruction / minimisation*, not a minimum-retention period → **P7-I3 wrong**; a staff-training requirement is not a DPIA/DPO obligation → **P7-I4 wrong**.
- **Note:** the sweep surfaced the *National Driver Licence Facial Recognition System* separately — defined **within** this Act; same law, not a second find.

### 🟢 Privacy Amendment (Public Health Contact Information) Act 2020 — new amendment Act
- **Register ID:** `C2020A00044` — https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2020A00044/latest/text
- **Independently verified (Federal Register API):** title confirmed; made 2020-05-15; **`isInForce: True`**, but **`isPrincipal: false`** — this is an **amendment Act**, not a standalone new statute. It inserted the COVIDSafe-app-data protections (Part VIIIA, incl. s 94ZC) **into the Privacy Act 1988** (which is already in the KNOWN baseline). Present it as a *new amendment*, not a new law.
- **Quote (Privacy Act 1988 s 94ZC, as inserted; tool-extracted):** _"COVID app data is the property of the Commonwealth, and remains the property of the Commonwealth even after it is disclosed to, or used by: (a) a State or Territory health authority; or (b) any other person or body …"_
- **Indicator mapping — NOT established by this quote.** The captured text is about *data ownership/control*; it does not by itself evidence a specific P7 indicator. The Act's P7-relevant substance (strict access limits → P7-I5 territory; mandatory deletion after the pandemic → P7-I3 territory) sits in **other** sections. **A reviewer must pull the specific access-restriction / deletion provision as evidence** before any indicator is cited; the sweep's "P7-I3" tag is not supported by the ownership quote.

### 🟡 Review (not counted as new-law finds)
- **Privacy Act 1988** and **My Health Records Act 2012** — already in the KNOWN baseline; the sweep matched *new provisions* of them (correctly demoted).
- **My Health Records Amendment (Strengthening Privacy) Act 2018** — real amendment, but the sweep's evidence is only the title and its URL resolves to the base 2012 Act; needs a clean re-pull before it can be relied on.

### 🇹🇭 Thailand — 2 verified-to-exist new subordinate laws (indicator TBC by a Thai-reading reviewer)
The PDPA and Cybersecurity Act B.E. 2562 themselves reconcile as KNOWN (#147).
Beyond them, the sweep surfaced two **Royal Decrees (พระราชกฤษฎีกา) that exempt
entities from the PDPA** — absent from the RDTII Round 2 baseline (its Thai Royal
Decrees are different ones: digital identification, digital-platform services,
customs tariff):

1. **Royal Decree prescribing agencies/businesses whose data controllers are not
   subject to the PDPA B.E. 2562** — _"พระราชกฤษฎีกากำหนดหน่วยงานและกิจการที่ผู้ควบคุมข้อมูลส่วนบุคคลไม่อยู่ภายใต้บังคับแห่งพระราชบัญญัติคุ้มครองข้อมูลส่วนบุคคล พ.ศ. 2562"_
2. **Royal Decree prescribing businesses/agencies exempted from applying parts of
   the PDPA B.E. 2562** — _"พระราชกฤษฎีกากำหนดลักษณะ กิจการ หรือหน่วยงานที่ได้รับการยกเว้นไม่ให้นำพระราชบัญญัติคุ้มครองข้อมูลส่วนบุคคล พ.ศ. 2562 บางส่วนมาใช้บังคับ"_

- **Existence independently confirmed** (DLA Piper *Data Protection Laws of the
  World — Thailand*): Thailand issued a **Royal Decree B.E. 2563 (2020)**
  exempting certain data controllers from PDPA applicability, and a **Royal Decree
  of 17 Aug 2023** exempting specified government authorities (NACC, Revenue,
  Customs, Excise Departments) from certain PDPA obligations. Decree (1) matches
  the 2020 decree; (2) is a partial-exemption decree — **a Thai-reading reviewer
  should confirm which registered decree each title is and pull the operative
  section.**
- **Absent from baseline:** ✔ (`is_known_law` = false; the baseline's Thai Royal
  Decrees are unrelated).
- **Indicator — assessment only, NOT yet evidenced by a quote.** Exemptions
  define the *scope* of the data-protection framework → **P7-I1 territory**; the
  2023 government-authority exemption also touches **P7-I5** (government handling
  of personal data). No verbatim provision was extracted (the large Thai PDFs
  stalled LLM extraction), so **do not cite an indicator until the operative
  section is pulled.**

## Method & honesty note

The KNOWN baseline is comprehensive, so genuine new Pillar 6/7 laws are scarce —
and that is the finding, not a failure. The pipeline was hardened (#138–#147) so
it would **not pad the NEW list** with an economy's own flagship statute, trade
agreements, mislabels, off-topic legislation, or over-tagged indicators.

Verified leads so far: **2 in Australia** (Identity Verification Services Act
2023; Privacy Amendment (Public Health Contact Information) Act 2020) and **2 in
Thailand** (the PDPA exemption Royal Decrees). Each was checked against an
authoritative source (Federal Register API for AUS; DLA Piper for THA) and the
baseline. **Where a claim could not be independently verified — an exact section,
an indicator mapping — it is flagged as such, not asserted.** A short, correct
list is worth more than a long one a reviewer cannot trust.

Reproduce (AUS): `RDTII_LLM_PROVIDER=deepseek python tools/discover_new_laws.py --economy Australia --pillar all --limit 35`
