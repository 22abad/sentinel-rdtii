# NEW-law discovery — vetted candidates for legal review (Pillars 6 & 7)

_Generated by `tools/discover_new_laws.py` (live discovery + mapping), then
reconciled against the KNOWN baseline (ESCAP RDTII Legal Inventory + local law
library) and triaged with `classify_new_candidate`. Each 🟢 lead was
additionally checked by hand: the law name was confirmed against the official
register, the source URL matches the named law, and the quote is on-topic for
the mapped indicator._

**A NEW find is a lead for attorney verification, not a settled legal
conclusion** (the project's draft-for-review guardrail). Katie / the trade-law
mentor makes the final call.

## Verdict legend
- 🟢 **CANDIDATE** — on-topic domestic statute, on an official domain, absent from the KNOWN baseline. A genuine new-law lead.
- 🟡 **REVIEW** — needs a human check (a new provision of an already-KNOWN law, or off an official domain).
- 🔴 **REJECT** — international agreement, LLM mislabel, or off-topic (kept in the JSON report for auditability, not listed here).

## 🇦🇺 Australia — 2 verified new-law leads

### 🟢 Identity Verification Services Act 2023
- **Register ID / source:** `C2023A00115` — https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2023A00115/latest/text ✅ title confirmed
- **Indicators:** P7-I1 (comprehensive data-protection framework), P7-I3 (retention), P7-I4 (DPO/DPIA), P7-I5 (government access to personal data)
- **Why it scores:** Establishes the statutory regime governing the Commonwealth's identity-verification services (the successor framework to the identity-matching arrangements). Directly on P7.5 — conditions and limits on government access to and disclosure of identification information.
- **Representative quote:** _"A participation agreement must provide for each party to the agreement that proposes to request identity verification services: (a) not to disclose identification information…"_
- **Absent from baseline:** ✔ (not in the ESCAP inventory or library)
- **Note:** the sweep also surfaced the *National Driver Licence Facial Recognition System* as a separate entry — it is defined **within** this Act, so treat it as the same law, not a second find.

### 🟢 Privacy Amendment (Public Health Contact Information) Act 2020
- **Register ID / source:** `C2020A00044` — https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2020A00044/latest/text ✅ title confirmed
- **Indicators:** P7-I3 (data-retention / deletion requirements)
- **Why it scores:** The COVIDSafe data-protection statute — imposes strict rules on retention, deletion and permitted use of COVID app data, and restricts government/law-enforcement access. A clean, dated P7.3 example.
- **Representative quote:** _"COVID app data is the property of the Commonwealth, and remains the property of the Commonwealth even after it is disclosed to, or used by: (a) a State or Territory…"_
- **Absent from baseline:** ✔

### 🟡 Review (not counted as new-law finds)
- **Privacy Act 1988** and **My Health Records Act 2012** — already in the KNOWN baseline; the sweep matched *new provisions* of them (correctly demoted).
- **My Health Records Amendment (Strengthening Privacy) Act 2018** — real amendment, but the sweep's evidence is only the title and its URL resolves to the base 2012 Act; needs a clean re-pull before it can be relied on.

## 🇸🇬 Singapore — no new-law lead beyond the baseline

The sweep surfaced only (a) international agreements (SAFTA, the Digital Economy
Agreements), (b) off-topic statutes (Fire Safety Act, Petroleum Act), and (c) a
new *provision* mislabelled onto the KNOWN PDPA 2012 (its source URL was the
Financial Services Act). **No genuine new P6/P7 domestic law** — the ESCAP
inventory + library already cover Singapore's framework (PDPA, Cybersecurity
Act, Spam Control Act, ETA, etc.).

## 🇲🇾 Malaysia — no new-law lead beyond the baseline

All candidates were either the CPTPP/RCEP trade agreements or the Malaysia PDPA
2010 relabelled with foreign law names (Hong Kong PDPO, Singapore PDPA) by the
extractor. **No genuine new P6/P7 domestic law** — the baseline already covers
the PDPA 2010, Cyber Security Act 2024 and the Communications & Multimedia Act.

## Method & honesty note

The KNOWN baseline for the three mandatory economies is comprehensive, so
genuine new Pillar 6/7 laws are scarce — and that is the finding, not a failure.
The pipeline was hardened (#138–#141) specifically so it would **not pad the NEW
list** with an economy's own flagship statute, trade agreements, mislabels, or
off-topic legislation. Two defensible AUS leads that survive that filter are
worth more than a hundred rows a reviewer cannot trust.

Reproduce: `RDTII_LLM_PROVIDER=deepseek python tools/discover_new_laws.py --economy Australia --pillar all --limit 35`
